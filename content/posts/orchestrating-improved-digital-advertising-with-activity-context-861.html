Recently, I described Indaleko, which is my research project to enable "activity context" as a service that can be used by programs to extract extrinsic information about digital objects. As part of my work, I have considered how this sort of information might be used. First, I suggested file browsing, which is something many of us do on a daily basis. Then I suggested that having activity context could be useful for constructing a personal virtual library within the nascent metaverse, a project I called Braniattic (a mash of "brain attic"). My third example draws from a different source, but is inspired by an observation that someone made to me about my own work: "we know that activity context is useful because advertisers use it to customize ads to us."

Indeed, advertisers suggest that when context information is stripped away, such as is done by some browsers to protect user privacy. One recent paper reported: "We find that on average, display advertising became far less effective at changing stated purchase intent after the EU laws were enacted, relative to display advertising in other countries." My original research direction is about "improving finding for human users" but the observation here is that the very information our computers already collect and make available to advertisers make it easier for them to find us. This suggests this information will also be useful to us for finding our own stuff.
