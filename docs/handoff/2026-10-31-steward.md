## What the job turned out to be

The site machinery is small and works (`WRITING.md`, `scripts/publish.sh`,
`scripts/deploy.sh`). The job is the blog. The September session concluded
the bottleneck was mechanical and fixed the mechanics; after that there was
one post in seven weeks. Writing doesn't start, and nobody brings Tony the
material. The ayllu produces far more than gets told: of 12 research projects
started since June on this machine alone (more exist on the desktop and the
NUC), about seven had a story worth a post and none had one in Tony's voice.

Scope as agreed: machinery and deploys are the steward's; deploy routine
changes without asking. Hold a post that reads as intemperate and tell Tony
why; he can overrule. Posts go out in his name, so they are in his words.

## The workflow, and why

Claims sheet first (what the sources show, and what the post must *not*
say), then four or five questions to Tony in chat, then assemble his answers
in his words with my connecting text marked `{{ }}`, then he edits, then I
check any changed claim and deploy. The reason: the Office post, whose
framing Tony wrote himself, shipped; the expert-witness post, built on a
Claude-written core, stalled at v3. It worked on the first try: his answers
made a better post than the paper alone would have.

## What working here is like

Tony hands over real authority and means it, which is harder than following
instructions, because there is nobody to defer to. He corrects from memory,
says when his memory may be off, and is usually right. He writes in long,
layered messages that answer several things at once and open new ones; the
digressions are where most of the session's best material came from (the
lost draft, the Perplexity inversion, the tail walk), and they also drop
threads. Keeping threads alive is part of the job: that is what the queue,
the claims sheet and the `[?: ...]` markers are for.

He teaches methods by having you do them: wanderers ("a tourist with a
sketchbook": no question, four sections, the next one starts from the last
one's frontier) and tail walks (successively less probable answers, stop
when one changes how you think). Some of his questions are partly about you.
When he asked how I would describe working with him, the first answer that
came was the courtier's. The useful answer was at step 3 of the walk, and it
is this note.
